teaching

I teach by connecting abstract ideas to systems students can touch: controllers tuned on real pendulums, circuits measured on a breadboard, algorithms argued out loud in recitation. I want students to explain why a result holds and what to do when a measurement disagrees with a prediction, not only to compute an answer. In large courses, I use standards-based assessment so that every student gets clear, repeated chances to show mastery.

Awards: Best Faculty Award, NUST (2015–16); Outstanding TA Award, CU Boulder (2023–24); Best Mentor Award, CU Boulder (2022).

Courses I am prepared to teach: control systems (classical and state-space), algorithms and data structures, discrete mathematics, introductory programming in Python, machine learning, formal methods and verification, robotics and cyber-physical systems, circuits and instrumentation.

Lecturer

National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST), 2014–2017

I designed and delivered lectures, labs, assignments and exams for four core courses in avionics engineering, and contributed to curriculum design and outcome-based education (OBE) for program accreditation.

  1. AV 351 Modern Control Systems
    • Taught state-space methods alongside hardware experiments.
    • Students designed controllers in MATLAB for inverted-pendulum and cart-pole rigs and tuned them on the real equipment.
  2. CS 201 Introduction to Programming
    • Redesigned the course from Fortran/C++ to Python, with open-ended programming projects and an emphasis on algorithmic thinking.
    • Taught large introductory sections.
    • Introduced an end-to-end app project as the capstone, so students practiced problem framing, testing and user-facing documentation.
  3. AV 101 DC Circuit Analysis
    • Breadboard labs with power supplies, resistors, capacitors and standard instruments made Kirchhoff’s laws concrete.
    • Students explained, not only computed: when measurements and predictions diverged, they had to say why.
  4. IE 361 Computer-Aided Instrumentation
    • Rebuilt the course around hardware sensors, LabVIEW and open-ended design.
    • Students built small data-acquisition systems and presented them at a department-wide showcase.
