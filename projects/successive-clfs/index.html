Successive CLFs

Chaining control Lyapunov functions to reach a goal through clutter.

Back to research

Driving a system to a goal through clutter rarely admits a single polynomial control Lyapunov function. Successive CLFs split the job: each certificate only has to bring the system into the region of a lower level, and the last one brings it home.

level 0 (goal) to level 10 not certified wall

A toy model, built so the mechanism is visible: a point robot with bounded speed in a room with one wall, and quadratic CLFs whose regions are the ellipses. Each region avoids the wall and contains the centre of the next-lower one. Click a free spot to launch a robot. The chart plots its active Lyapunov function, which decreases inside each level and restarts lower at every hand-over. In the method itself, the CLFs are polynomials for nonlinear systems, synthesized with SOS programming.

The idea

A control Lyapunov function \(V\) certifies that a system \(\dot x = f(x, u)\) can be driven to a goal: \(V\) is positive away from the goal, and wherever it applies there is an input with

\[\nabla V(x) \cdot f(x, u) \;\le\; -\alpha\, V(x).\]

On a cluttered domain, the region where one polynomial \(V\) can meet this condition while steering clear of obstacles is small.

Successive CLFs use a sequence \(V_0, V_1, \dots, V_N\) with regions \(\mathcal{D}_k = \{x : V_k(x) \le 1\}\). The base certificate \(V_0\) is an ordinary CLF around the goal. For \(k \ge 1\), \(V_k\) only needs to decrease on \(\mathcal{D}_k\) outside the lower regions, and its closed-loop trajectories must enter \(\mathcal{D}_0 \cup \dots \cup \mathcal{D}_{k-1}\) without becoming unsafe. Every level is a separate SOS program, so the hierarchy grows outward from the goal one certificate at a time.

At run time, the controller selects the lowest level whose region contains the current state and follows that level’s CLF until a lower level takes over. Levels only go down, so each level is used at most once and there are at most \(N\) switches; per-level transit times add up to a bound on the time to reach the goal (Wajid, 2026).

What to look for in the demo

  • A robot starting at the far end of the room passes through every level in order; its trail changes colour at each hand-over.
  • In the chart, each tooth of the sawtooth is one level’s \(V_k\) running down. It never rises within a level.
  • The hatched corners are states no level certifies. Adding levels, or richer polynomial certificates, shrinks them.
