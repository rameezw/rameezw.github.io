Repulsive barriers

One certificate, synthesized in the obstacle's frame, reused wherever the obstacle goes.

Back to research

Moving obstacles normally force a certificate that depends on time or on the obstacle's state. For vehicles whose dynamics don't change when you shift them in space, one certificate synthesized around an obstacle at the origin works for an obstacle anywhere: re-express the vehicle relative to where the obstacle is.

The idea

Split the state into a position \(p \in \mathbb{R}^2\) and everything else, \(\xi\) (heading, speed, angular rate, …). For car-like and aircraft-like models, the dynamics don’t depend on where the vehicle is:

\[\dot p = g(\xi, u), \qquad \dot \xi = h(\xi, u).\]

They are unchanged by translations \(p \mapsto p - c\). So if \(B(p, \xi)\) is a barrier certificate for an obstacle centred at the origin, then \(B(p - c, \xi)\) is a barrier certificate for the same obstacle centred at \(c\). Nothing has to be re-synthesized when the obstacle moves.

To handle motion, the supervisor samples the obstacle’s centre \(\hat c\) every \(\tau\) seconds and recentres. Between samples the true centre drifts by at most the obstacle’s speed times \(\tau\); that error enters the synthesis as a bounded disturbance, so the certificate stays valid until the next sample. As with successive barriers, the method synthesizes a family of barriers, each paired with a fixed input, and the supervisor passes the nominal controller until the active certificate enters a band near its boundary, then overrides with that certificate’s input (Wajid et al., 2026).
